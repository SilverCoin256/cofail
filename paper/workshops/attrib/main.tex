\begin{abstract}
Contributive and corroborative attribution answer ``which training data explains this output,''
one output at a time, and need access to training data or model internals that is routinely
unavailable for open leaderboard submissions. This idea-track paper describes a training-data-free,
population-scale signal available even when neither is. After conditioning on the exact,
fit-free null implied by Rasch sufficiency, $1{,}228$--$1{,}362$ open models per benchmark share
residual failure correlation $2.9$--$11.9\times$ its null, concentrated in $4$--$12$ latent
dimensions rather than one shared factor, and the signal survives removing $21$--$64\%$ of models
as near-duplicates. We tried to decompose it against declared lineage and could not: a
pre-registered gate found a declared parent model for only $23.3\%$ of models, below the $40\%$
threshold set in advance, so the analysis was dropped rather than run on a biased subset. We
think that failure is itself the useful datum for this venue: it is a concrete, quantified
instance of the provenance-opacity problem this community studies, and we pose the residual
correlation matrix, together with the coarse family labels we do have, as a candidate for what an
aggregate, black-box provenance signal could look like when nothing about training data is
disclosed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The gap we are pointing at}

Contributive attribution estimates the causal effect of specific training examples on a specific
output \citep{kohliang2017,park2023trak}; corroborative attribution finds sources that support a
specific output \citep{worledge2024}. Both operate at the level of one model and one output.
Neither is designed for the regime of an open leaderboard with thousands of independently
submitted models, most of undisclosed lineage, where the question is not ``which example explains
this generation'' but ``which models share unstated ancestry with which others, and can that be
seen at all without the training data.''

We met this question from the measurement side, while characterising correlated failure between
open language models on standard multiple-choice benchmarks. We report what we found, and what we
could not do, because the gap between what we needed and what exists seems to us a useful data
point for this workshop even though we do not close it.

\section{A population-scale, training-data-free residual signal}

Let $F_{im}=1$ iff model $i$ fails item $m$, with $c_m$ the number of models failing item $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Mean co-failure depends only on item margins]
\label{lem:mean}
$O = \frac{1}{MN(N-1)}\sum_m(c_m^2-c_m)$; hence any randomization preserving the item margins
leaves the mean pairwise co-failure rate $O$ exactly invariant, with zero variance.
\end{lemma}

The row and column margins of $F$ are the jointly sufficient statistics of the Rasch model, so
conditioning on both, sampled with curveball \citep{strona2014} and verified against exhaustive
enumeration on small fibres, gives an exact, fit-free null under which mean-level co-failure is
provably uninformative about shared behaviour (Lemma~\ref{lem:mean}; the same degeneracy is
Schluter's V-ratio in ecology and Cronbach's $\alpha$ in psychometrics
\citep{schluter1984,gotelli2000,cronbach1951}). At that null, on the archived Open LLM Leaderboard
(five benchmarks, $1{,}228$--$1{,}362$ models each, harvested from result files at zero inference
cost), the residual correlation that remains after the difficulty degeneracy is removed is
$2.9$--$11.9\times$ its null and spectrally concentrated: on ARC-Challenge, six eigenvalues exceed
the null's spectral edge and the leading eigenvalue carries $54\%$ of squared residual mass, the
signature of several latent dimensions rather than one shared factor. This is not a
duplicate-submission artifact. Clustering models by pairwise agreement and keeping one
representative per cluster at threshold $0.90$ removes $900$ of $1{,}362$ ARC models and moves the
participation ratio of the residual spectrum from $23.6$ only to $29.0$ (its exact-margin null
falls from $449.6$ to $289.3$); recomputing every statistic at threshold $0.95$, which discards
$21$--$64\%$ of each benchmark's models with the Rasch fit and the null redrawn, moves the rms
excess ratio by at most $1.14\times$ on every benchmark (ARC $5.58\to5.44$). Whatever produces this
structure survives the crudest form of provenance filtering available to us.

That last clause is doing real work: it is the crudest form available because we do not have a
better one. We do not observe pretraining corpora, fine-tuning data, or distillation relationships
for the large majority of these models. Two attribution routes were tried. The leaderboard's
optional, self-reported \texttt{base\_model} field resolved a declared parent for $317$ of
$1{,}362$ ARC models ($23.3\%$), with $315$ model repositories no longer retrievable at all; we had
pre-registered a $40\%$ coverage threshold below which a lineage decomposition would not be
trusted, so it was dropped rather than reported on a biased subset. Name-string matching against
known family names attributes $34.5$--$35.7\%$ of model identifiers per benchmark, with roughly
$125$ models per benchmark carrying merge-related names and the five largest named families
(Mistral, Llama-2, Llama-3, Mixtral, and a fifth that varies) covering only $25$--$26\%$. We are
not claiming the residual correlation \emph{is} shared provenance; the honest statement is
narrower and, we think, more useful to this audience: standard attribution machinery cannot
currently tell us either way, because it needs exactly the access this population does not
provide.

\section{Why this might be attribution's problem, not measurement's}

Three properties of the residual structure make it look more like a provenance signal than a
behavioural coincidence, and we label the inference that follows as a conjecture. First, it is not
explained by discrimination heterogeneity: a two-parameter item-response simulation with
$\mathrm{sd}(\log a)=0.60$ yields rms residual correlation $0.076$ against an observed $0.248$ on
ARC. Second, it is not explained by literal duplicate submissions, as above. Third, its
dimensionality is benchmark-specific and broadly stable under deduplication ($4$--$6$ dimensions
above the null's spectral edge on four benchmarks, $12$ on HellaSwag). \emph{Conjecture:} this is
the structure one would expect from a handful of dominant pretraining lineages or shared
distillation teachers producing correlated errors along a few behavioural axes, rather than from
one dominant lineage or from fully independent training runs. None of this is proof, and our own
simulations show that a population with two ability dimensions and no shared lineage reproduces
much of the effect ($\lambda_1^2/\sum\lambda_k^2=0.457$ against the observed $0.540$), so
unmodelled ability dimensions remain an alternative we cannot exclude. What we can say is that the
exact conditional null gives a population-scale test that is invariant to item difficulty by
construction, costs no training-data access, and is sensitive to structure at a scale (thousands
of models) at which per-example causal attribution is not, to our knowledge, currently run.

\section{An idea-track question}

We pose this as a question rather than a method, and it is now concretely testable with the coarse
labels above: do the leading residual eigenvectors of a margin-conditioned co-failure matrix
separate, even loosely, the Mistral-7B-derived population from the Llama-2-derived one, or the
merge-named models from the rest? If they do, the residual matrix would be a training-data-free,
black-box complement to contributive and corroborative attribution, useful precisely in the regime
where those methods' preconditions (access to training data; an identified individual output to
explain) fail, such as a regulatory or journalistic audit of a leaderboard's actual diversity of
origin. If they do not, and the residual structure is better explained by convergent training
choices than by shared lineage, that is itself a useful negative result about how much
population-scale behavioural correlation should ever be read as a provenance signal. We do not know
which is true; we have not run the eigenvector-versus-family comparison, and we think this venue
is the right place to decide whether it is worth running.

\section{Limitations}

This paper reports a measurement and a gap, not an attribution method; we performed no causal or
corroborative attribution. Discrimination heterogeneity is ruled out as a sufficient explanation
but unmodelled ability dimensions are not. The name-string family labels are a lower bound on
lineage, not ground truth. Claims are restricted to the evaluated open-model ecosystem on
multiple-choice benchmarks. Code, data, and the pre-registration with dated amendments will be
linked in the camera-ready. A large language model was used as a coding and drafting assistant;
the authors specified and pre-registered the hypotheses, verified every derivation and number
against executed-run artifacts, and are responsible for the content.
