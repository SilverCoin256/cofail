\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries How Many Independent Models Does an Open-Model Ecosystem Contain?}\\[3pt]
{\small\color{muted} One-page research brief \quad\textbullet\quad Shaurya Gupta \quad\textbullet\quad
\href{https://github.com/SilverCoin256/cofail}{github.com/SilverCoin256/cofail}}
\end{center}
\vspace{4pt}

\noindent\textbf{The question.} If many independently developed AI models are deployed as an
ensemble, a judging panel, or competing screening vendors, how much of that plurality is real?
The standard evidence — how often pairs of models fail on the same test item, versus a baseline
assuming independence — cannot answer this, for a provable reason.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{The result.} The mean co-failure rate across a model population is a function of
\emph{item difficulty alone}; it is invariant under any randomization that preserves that
difficulty, so its excess over such a baseline is exactly zero, not merely small. That degeneracy
turns out to be a known result independently in three fields (ecology's V-ratio, psychometrics'
Cronbach's $\alpha$, Fleiss's rater agreement) that had never been connected to AI evaluation.
Conditioning on the one \emph{exact, fit-free} null available — the distribution implied by the
Rasch model's own sufficient statistics, requiring no estimated parameters — removes that
artifact and leaves a real second-order signal: residual correlation \textbf{5.5$\times$} the
null on the primary benchmark (\textbf{2.9--11.9$\times$} across all five), concentrated in
\textbf{roughly six} extra latent dimensions, across \textbf{1{,}228--1{,}373 open models
per benchmark} on five benchmarks, harvested at \$0 compute cost from public evaluation logs.
The exact-null sampler itself is validated by direct enumeration (a $\chi^2$ test against
uniformity on fibres up to size 1{,}170 does not reject), and its detection power against
planted alternatives is characterised rather than assumed — including one alternative it has
\emph{no} power against by construction, stated as a property of the design rather than a
caveat.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{What makes this a research process rather than a result.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=14pt,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item A pre-registered primary hypothesis came back with an inconsistent sign across benchmarks.
Its kill condition, written in advance, called that a refutation --- reported as refuted, not
reinterpreted.
\item An adversarial review, run deliberately against the project's own headline finding before
submission, found that "$\sim$1{,}300 models behave like $\sim$24 independent ones" is
\href{https://github.com/SilverCoin256/cofail/blob/main/results/RESULTS_DIGEST.md}{algebraically
incapable} of distinguishing one weak shared factor from many tight clusters. \textbf{The claim
was withdrawn} and replaced with the diagnostics that can tell the two apart.
\item A closely related paper turned up in a deliberate prior-art search on the same archive.
Rather than abandon the direction, the project narrowed to the specific open question that paper
leaves unanswered.
\item A separate pre-registered test, aimed at a published claim in prior work, came back against
the project's own hypothesis: the claim survives conditioning almost fully (88\% of the effect
remains) and is reported as robust, exactly as committed to in advance.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{What keeps happening after this document.} A scheduled process re-checks the
public archive monthly, on GitHub's own infrastructure, and appends a dated row to a public time
series --- the same frozen method applied to more data, not a new claim.
